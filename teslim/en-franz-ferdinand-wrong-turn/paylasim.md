Franz Ferdinand's driver wasn't told the route had changed

On 28 June 1914, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife Sophie visited Sarajevo, where a wrong turn put their car in a fateful spot.

Earlier that morning, an explosive device thrown at the motorcade bounced off the archduke's open car and exploded under the next one, wounding several people. After a reception at city hall, Franz Ferdinand decided to visit the officers hurt in the blast at the hospital.

The plan was changed so the cars would go straight along the Appel Quay by the river. According to the Austrian State Archives and other accounts, the drivers were not properly informed.

At the corner by the Latin Bridge, the lead car turned into Franz Joseph Street, and the archduke's driver, Leopold Lojka, followed it. The governor, General Oskar Potiorek, who rode in the car, called out that they were going the wrong way. Lojka stopped to reverse, and for a few seconds the car stood still beside Schiller's delicatessen, close to where the assassin Gavrilo Princip was standing. Franz Ferdinand and Sophie did not survive the day.

The car, a 1910 Gräf & Stift, is now in a Vienna museum. The delicatessen building is today the Museum of Sarajevo. The background shows the Latin Bridge as it looks now. Historians have found no contemporary evidence that Princip was eating a sandwich there.

The wojak characters are fictional and their lines are dramatized.

Was the wrong turn fate, or just one more mistake that day?
