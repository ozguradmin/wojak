The Man Who Stole the Mona Lisa Tried to Sell Her Back to Italy

The theft of the Mona Lisa in 1911 was carried out by Vincenzo Peruggia, a former worker at the Louvre. On a Monday in August 1911, he walked in through a staff entrance at about 7 a.m., by his own account. He took the painting out of its frame and carried it away wrapped in his work smock.

For more than two years he kept the painting hidden in Paris. In November 1913 he wrote to the Florentine dealer Alfredo Geri, signing himself "Leonard V.", and offered to sell the painting to Italy. Later accounts say he asked for 500,000 lire.

On 11 December, Geri and Giovanni Poggi, director of the Uffizi, met him at the Hotel Tripoli in Florence. Poggi examined the painting at the Uffizi, and police were informed. Peruggia was arrested on 12 December. In his hotel room, police found a wooden box with a false bottom.

The painting was shown in Florence, Rome and Milan before it returned to the Louvre on 4 January 1914.

Peruggia said he had acted out of patriotism, believing Napoleon had taken the work from Italy. The court did not accept that motive. In June 1914 he was sentenced to one year and 15 days; on appeal the term was cut to seven months and eight days, which he had already served. Peruggia passed away near Paris in 1925.

- [ ] **Dramatization:** the two wojak characters are unnamed; the dialogue is invented and the pinned comment says so.
- [ ] **Dates:** the arrest is 12 December 1913 (Florence police card); 11 December is the meeting at the hotel. Some popular sources give 11 December for the arrest; we do not.
- [ ] **Letter:** the signature on the scanned letter is 'Leonard V.'; 'Leonardo Vincenzo' is a later expansion and is not used. The 500,000 lire sum is reported, not documented, so it stays in the story text as 'later accounts say'.
- [ ] **Licence:** both evidence images are public domain (1913; US and Commons tags). The Uffizi photo credit is The Telegraph; its original publication was not checked. The 1909 Paris mugshot was left out because its identity and date are not fully confirmed.
- [ ] **Character style:** Peruggia's tan jacket and bib overalls read as a 1920s-30s farmhand; Louvre workers wore white smocks. Known weakness, see RAPOR. Geri's fedora is a little later than 1913.
- [ ] **Face variant:** peruggia_sok is a hand-edited face (eyes, brows, mouth) on the library body, not a ready-made face; the visual judge must check it at phone size.
