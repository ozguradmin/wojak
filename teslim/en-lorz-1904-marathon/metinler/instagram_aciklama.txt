Roosevelt's Daughter Crowned the Wrong 1904 Olympic Winner

The men's marathon at the 1904 Olympics in St. Louis, Missouri, was run on August 30 over dusty country roads in the afternoon heat. Only 14 of the 32 starters finished.

The first man across the line, in about 3 hours 13 minutes, was Fred Lorz, a 20-year-old bricklayer from New York. The crowd cheered him as the winner, and he reportedly posed for a photo with Alice Roosevelt, the President's daughter, who placed a wreath on his head. She was about to give him the gold medal when someone spoke up.

Lorz had cramped at about nine miles and dropped out. He then reportedly rode in a car for roughly eleven miles, until it broke down about five miles from the stadium, and he rejoined the race on foot. Sources disagree on whose car it was and how far he rode. Lorz said it was only a joke. He was disqualified, and the Amateur Athletic Union banned him for life; the ban was soon reduced, and he won the Boston Marathon the next year.

The official winner was Thomas Hicks, whose trainers reportedly gave him strychnine, egg whites and brandy along the way. His time was 3:28:53.

The first evidence frame shows the start of the race. The last frame is captioned "Following the Marathon Race" in Charles Lucas's 1905 book and shows a car with race officials aboard; it does not show Lorz.
