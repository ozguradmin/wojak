The Ghost Army: the WWII unit that fought with illusions

In September 1944, a secret US Army unit spent about a week in the woods near Bettembourg, Luxembourg, pretending to be an armored division. It was the 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, later nicknamed the Ghost Army: roughly 1,100 men, many of them artists and sound engineers, who fought with illusions instead of firepower. Their tools: inflatable tanks, recorded engine noise, fake radio.

Operation Bettembourg ran from 15 to 22 September 1944. Patton's Third Army was attacking Metz, and about 25 miles of nearby front were held thinly, by a single cavalry squadron. The real 6th Armored Division was still far away, so, according to the memorial at the site and the Ghost Army Legacy Project, roughly 400 to 500 men played it, a force of about 8,000 on paper.

About a dozen inflatable Shermans went up the first night, but they reportedly came down after about 24 hours, once a civilian had spotted four soldiers carrying one; an M4 decoy weighed about 93 pounds. Sound trucks, fake radio and play-acting did much of the work. No German record confirming that it worked has surfaced; the claim comes mainly from the unit's own reports.

The unit stayed classified until 1996. In 2022 Congress passed a law awarding it the Congressional Gold Medal, presented in 2024.

Frames: the site's memorial marker, then a replica dummy tank at the International Spy Museum. Dramatized: characters fictional, dialogue invented.

Would you have fallen for a 93-pound tank, or only for the sound of one?
