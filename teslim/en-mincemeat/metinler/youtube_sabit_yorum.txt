Operation Mincemeat was a British deception of 1943, and it began with a body.

Glyndwr Michael, a homeless Welshman, passed away in January 1943. British intelligence dressed him as Major William Martin of the Royal Marines, gave him a forged identity card and personal letters, and chained a briefcase of fake invasion letters to him. In late April 1943 he was released from the submarine HMS Seraph off the coast of Spain, and a fisherman found him at Huelva on 30 April.

Spain passed the papers to German intelligence. Hitler moved forces to Greece and Sardinia. In July 1943 the Allies invaded Sicily, the target the plan had tried to hide.

The idea is widely attributed to a 1939 memo, linked to Ian Fleming, which borrowed a scheme from a 1937 crime novel. The photo on the identity card shows an MI5 officer, not Michael. Michael's real name was only added to Martin's headstone in 1997.

The memorial plaque in Aberbargoed, Wales, now carries his name. It prints 24 April 1943, the date on Martin's headstone. Records show he passed away in January.
