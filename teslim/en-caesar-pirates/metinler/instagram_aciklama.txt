Julius Caesar's Pirate Kidnappers Thought He Was Joking

Around 75 BC, a young Roman nobleman named Julius Caesar was sailing across the Aegean when Cilician pirates captured him near the island of Pharmacusa. They demanded a ransom of twenty talents. According to the Greek writer Plutarch, Caesar laughed at them for not knowing who their captive was and offered fifty instead.

He stayed with the pirates for about forty days while his followers collected the money in various cities. Plutarch writes that Caesar joined their games, read them his poems and speeches, and called them illiterate barbarians when they did not applaud. He also reportedly told them, often and with a laugh, that he would have them all crucified (Plutarch's translators say "hang"; Suetonius says crucify). The pirates took it for boyish high spirits.

Once the ransom was paid, Caesar manned ships at Miletus, even though he held no public office at the time. He found the pirates still at anchor off the island and captured most of them. When the governor of Asia dragged his feet over their fate, Caesar went to Pergamum and had them crucified, just as he had promised.

Ancient writers differ on some details, such as where Caesar was heading when he was taken. The dialogue is imagined and the pirate's hat is a meme shortcut. Images: the ruins of Miletus (background), a model trireme for illustration, and a silver denarius minted decades later that carries his name.
