The Palomares incident was a Cold War nuclear accident. On 17 January 1966, a US Air Force B-52 bomber and a KC-135 tanker collided during mid-air refuelling near the fishing village of Palomares in southern Spain. Seven airmen lost their lives. The B-52 carried four unarmed thermonuclear weapons. Three fell on land, and the conventional explosives in two of them detonated, scattering plutonium over farmland. The fourth fell into the Mediterranean.

A local fisherman, Francisco Simó Orts, told US officials he had seen a parachute come down into the sea. Reportedly, some Navy commanders doubted him at first, according to a search team member's later account. The submersible Alvin spotted the weapon in mid-March (15 March in official reports), about 2,550 feet down on a steep slope, and it was raised aboard the USS Petrel on 7 April, about 80 days after the collision.

Simó Orts then turned to the courts. Under maritime salvage law, a person who identifies where lost property lies can claim a share of its value. He claimed $5 million from the US. Accounts of what he finally received differ, from about $10,000 to about $14,500, and some say he was never paid in full. He passed away in 2003.

Plutonium contamination near Palomares was only partly cleaned up, and parts of the land there remain fenced off today.

The dialogue and characters are imagined. Who really found the weapon: the fisherman, the Navy, or both?
