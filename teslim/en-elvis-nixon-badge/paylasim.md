Elvis Asked President Nixon to Make Him a Federal Agent

The Elvis Presley–Richard Nixon meeting took place on December 21, 1970, after Presley arrived at the White House without an appointment. He had flown overnight from Los Angeles to Washington, and at about 6:30 in the morning he handed a handwritten letter to a guard at the northwest gate. Written on American Airlines stationery, it offered his help in the fight against narcotics and asked to be made a "Federal Agent at Large." He reportedly told the White House he was staying at a hotel under the name Jon Burrows.

A White House aide wrote a memo proposing that Presley be allowed to meet the president briefly. The chief of staff's reply, scrawled in the margin, was "You must be kidding", and yet the meeting was approved (the video's first evidence frame is part of page two of that memo).

Around midday Presley was shown into the Oval Office. He had brought a World War II Colt .45 pistol as a gift, but the Secret Service took it at the security check. A White House photographer, Ollie Atkins, captured the handshake. Before leaving the Oval Office, Presley hugged the president. According to later accounts, he also asked for a federal narcotics badge, and that afternoon an official from the federal narcotics bureau gave him one. Aides later described it as honorary.

The visit stayed secret until the Washington Post reported it in January 1972. Today the photograph is one of the most requested images in the National Archives.

Would you have let him in?
