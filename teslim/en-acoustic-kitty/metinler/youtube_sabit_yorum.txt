Acoustic Kitty is the name of a 1960s Central Intelligence Agency project that tried to turn a live house cat into a mobile listening device. A heavily redacted CIA memo from 1967, "Views on Trained Cats", has since been declassified. According to accounts of the program, surgeons reportedly placed a microphone in the cat's ear, a transmitter near its skull and a thin antenna in its fur, so it could wander near Soviet targets and transmit conversations.

Training was the hard part. Cats go where they please, and reportedly one wandered off when it was hungry or distracted. The memo says cats can indeed be trained to move short distances, but concludes that the method would not be practical in a real foreign setting. The program ended in 1967.

The best-known ending is less certain. A former CIA officer said that on its first field test, in Washington, D.C., the cat was hit by a taxi. A former CIA technical chief disputes that, and no declassified document settles it. The traffic scene in the video dramatizes that disputed account. Sources also disagree on the cost, with figures of 15 million dollars and more.

Both evidence frames are pages of the declassified CIA memo.
