Hitler's Deputy Rudolf Hess Parachuted Into Scotland

The Hess flight was a solo flight to Scotland on 10 May 1941 by Rudolf Hess, Adolf Hitler's deputy. He took off from Augsburg in a Messerschmitt Bf 110 and reportedly hoped to reach the Duke of Hamilton, whom he believed could open peace talks with Britain. Hess had never really met the Duke; a German academic had suggested him.

Aiming for the Duke's home, Dungavel House, he bailed out near Eaglesham, south of Glasgow, about 12 miles away, and hurt his ankle. A local ploughman found him and helped him into a farmhouse. He gave the name "Hauptmann Alfred Horn" and asked to be taken to the Duke. The Duke, an RAF officer, was on duty elsewhere that night. He met Hess the next day and had no interest in a deal. Berlin's official statement later blamed Hess's mental state.

Hess was held in Britain for the rest of the war, then sentenced to life at Nuremberg in 1946. From 1 October 1966 he was the only prisoner left in Spandau Prison in Berlin. He passed away on 17 August 1987, aged 93. The official finding was that he took his own life, which his family disputed. Some writers still debate whether Hitler knew about the flight in advance.
