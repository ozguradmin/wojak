In 1932, Australia sent soldiers to fight emus on wheat farms

The Emu War, as the press nicknamed it, was a 1932 military operation in the wheatbelt of Western Australia, around the Campion district. Farmers who were ex-soldiers had told the Defence Minister, Sir George Pearce, that ordinary firearms were useless against emus damaging crops and fences, because the birds scattered when shot at. They asked for Lewis automatic weapons. Reportedly about 20,000 emus had arrived in the wheat areas.

The government sent three soldiers, led by Major Meredith, reportedly with two Lewis automatic weapons and 10,000 rounds. The first attempts began on 2 November. On 4 November about 1,000 emus approached a dam, but one weapon jammed after roughly a dozen birds were shot. By 8 November about 2,500 rounds had reportedly been fired, estimates of birds shot range from about 50 to about 500, and the soldiers were withdrawn after press criticism.

On 9 November the House of Representatives was told that the weapons had been returned to Perth. After a report that 300 emus had been shot, the operation resumed in mid-November. Meredith later claimed 986 birds shot with 9,860 rounds, figures that are widely disputed, and he was recalled on 10 December. Bounties, in place since 1923, continued: 57,034 were claimed in six months of 1934.

The final frame is a recreation; the quote is from Hansard (House of Representatives), 9 November 1932.
